Dustimony™ User Guide

For Dustimony 1.0 on iPhone

Dustimony runs a cleaning business from your pocket: who you clean for, what gets done in each house, and what you are owed. Your book works with no signal, so a basement with no bars never stops your day. This guide walks the app the way you walk a work day.

Your clients never install anything. Texts come from your own phone number, and invoices arrive as normal PDFs. If you switch on card payments, they tap a link and pay on Stripe's secure page. To them it is just you, organized.

1. Get your clients in

Open the Clients tab and tap +. Add one client by hand, with their address and schedule on a single screen, or bring your whole list at once.

  • From Contacts: pick the people who are clients. Names, phones and addresses come across.
  • From a spreadsheet: a CSV out of Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. Only a name column is required and column order does not matter. You see a preview of exactly what will be created, with any problems flagged, before anything is saved.

Give a client a schedule (weekly, every two weeks, or monthly) and the coming weeks fill in by themselves. The list shows each client's cadence and what they owe, and the filters at the top split it into Active, Leads and Past due.

2. Your prices

Settings → Service catalog is your price list. It arrives filled in with the jobs most cleaners sell: standard, deep, move-in/move-out and post-construction, with a set of add-ons underneath. Every one is yours to rename, reprice or delete.

Two things read from this list. A new booking offers these services at these prices, and the add-ons become the chips you tap when you finish a job. Change a price here and future work uses it; work already completed keeps the price it was done at, because that number is history, not a setting.

3. Run your day

The Today tab is the working screen. Your stops are in time order with the address, the access note, and a directions button that opens Maps. The header counts what is left and what you have earned so far.

Tap Timer to start the clock when you walk in. If you forget to start it, or forget to stop it, you can type the real minutes at completion. The app never invents a number for you.

Running behind? Dustimony offers to text the clients who are still waiting, one message each, from your number. You tap send.

4. At the house

Tap a stop to open it. This is the screen you work from: how to get in, and what the client expects done.

  • The checklist is room by room, from the template on the property. Tick items as you go. It saves as you tap.
  • Access and alarm notes sit at the top, so a gate code or an alarm sequence is one tap away rather than a text you have to find.
  • Add for this visit only puts an extra item on today's list without changing the template for every future clean.

Door codes, key locations and alarm details are private. They live in your own iCloud, they are never written to a log, and they never leave the device unless you send them.

5. Complete a job

Complete opens the sheet where the job becomes money. The price is already filled in from the booking. Tap a chip to add an extra. The price on the chip is the one from your catalog, and the total moves as you tap.

The time is whatever the timer recorded, and you can correct it by typing the minutes or the start and end times. Notes are for the thing the word "notes" cannot say: the key that would not turn, the room you could not reach.

Done records the charge against the client. Nothing is sent to anyone at this point; the money side is yours to run when you choose.

6. The week

The Schedule tab shows the week a day at a time, with each day's count and what it is worth. Switch to Month for the shape of the month, where a dot marks a booked day.

Move a visit and the app asks whether you mean this one or the whole series. Take a holiday or a slow week off and those days clear for everyone, with the bookings picking up on the other side.

If a booking ever collides with another, the row says so with a warning, before you drive to it.

7. Text your clients

Settings → Message templates holds every message Dustimony writes: the day-before reminder, on my way, running late, the invoice text, and the three payment reminders. Tokens like {{name}} and {{time}} fill themselves in.

Arrival time decides what a client is told. Exact time sends "9:00 AM". An arrival window sends "between 9:00 and 11:00", which is a promise you can keep on a day that runs long. Your own screens always show the exact time.

Every message opens in your phone's Messages app with the text already written. Nothing is ever sent automatically, and it comes from your number, not ours.

8. Get paid

The Money tab opens on what you are owed, split by how old it is. Anything past thirty days is worth a nudge, and the reminder is already written.

Record cash, a check, Venmo, Zelle, Apple Pay or a card payment against a client in two taps. The balance updates as you do.

Invoices

An invoice gathers completed visits into one document. Send it as a text or share the PDF; both carry the same line items, and both carry a pay link if you have card payments switched on. An issued invoice never rewrites itself. Change a price afterwards and the ledger changes, while the document you already sent stays as it was.

Mark it paid when the check arrives, or let a recorded payment do it. If you issued one in error, void it: the charges go back to being billable, and the sales tax the invoice added comes back off.

Card payments

Card payments are optional and use Stripe. You connect your own free Stripe account, money lands in your bank, and Dustimony never sees a card number. Cards cost about 3.4% plus 30¢ per payment, which is Stripe's cut plus 0.5% to Dustimony. Cash and checks you record yourself cost nothing, and the app is free to use on your own.

9. Know your numbers

Money → Report answers what a week, a month, a quarter or a year actually did. Income is what you were paid, not what you billed, and the report says so on the screen. Under it: visits, hours worked, clients gained and lost, what came in by payment method, and what went out by category.

Page back with the arrows. Share it as a file when your accountant asks.

Expenses

Log anything the business spends, from supplies and fuel to equipment and insurance, with a vendor and an optional photo of the receipt. Where it went ranks the month by category, which is usually enough to see the leak.

If you run crew, they can log expenses from their phones and the receipt photo comes back to you.

10. Know your clients

A client's page carries their balance, their status, and one-tap text, call and directions. Underneath: what they have been worth all time and this year, their average visit length, and every visit you have ever done for them.

Mark a client paused for the season, or lost with the reason why. The reason is kept on the record, so the win-back list later is a list of facts rather than a guess.

11. Your crew

Crew is a subscription, $18.99 a month. Setting it up is free; you subscribe before anyone can sign in. It gives each cleaner a login on a website, with no app to install and no account for them to create.

Settings → Turn on crew creates your business code and adds your first cleaner with a name and a PIN. You choose what each person can see:

  • prices, on or off
  • client phone numbers, on or off
  • access and alarm notes, on or off
  • the rest of the week, or only today
  • whether they can log expenses

Anything a person is not allowed to see is never sent to the server in the first place, rather than hidden on the page.

What your cleaner sees

They open the crew website on their phone and sign in with the business code, their name and their PIN. No app, no password to reset, and it works on Android and iPhone alike. The page is in English or Spanish, their choice, on the sign-in screen.

Their day is a list of stops. Tapping one opens the room checklist, the timer, and, if you allowed it, the door code behind a button, so it is not sitting on screen in someone's hand at the supermarket.

What they tick, the time they log and the expenses they enter come back to you.

Teams and the roster

Two vans, two routes. Make a team in Settings → Teams, then use the team filter on the Schedule tab to see one team's week at a time. Roster, in that same filter menu, is where you set who rides with which team on which day. That changes weekly, so it lives with the schedule rather than in Settings.

12. Your business, your data

Everything is stored on your iPhone and synced through your own private iCloud, which we cannot read. Two devices signed into the same Apple account stay in step. With no iCloud account the app still works completely; it just stays on the one phone, and the Settings screen says so plainly rather than pretending.

Automatic backups write a full copy to your iCloud Drive. Back up everything to a file saves one anywhere you like, and a file you saved yourself survives anything that happens to the app. Export everything as CSV hands your clients and history to a spreadsheet, because it is your business and it should be portable.
